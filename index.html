Payment Controls

Duplicate Payment Detection & Recovery

My earliest project — done with nothing fancy.

This was one of the first projects where I realized I could use the tools I already had to solve a much bigger problem.

At the time, we were paying for third-party software to detect potential duplicate payments. The results were only available for review once a week, required significant adjustment before the data was practical to work with, and didn't give us the flexibility I wanted for day-to-day review.

Risk & Analytics

Miskey Invoice Risk Reporting

Data analytics at its finest.

Miskeys are one of the leading causes of the duplicate payments I see, but duplicate payments aren't the only risk. A small invoice-entry error can create payment problems, complicate vendor accounts, and cause issues much further down the road.

I wanted a way to find those errors without already knowing what I was looking for.

I developed reporting that analyzes invoice activity for unusual patterns and potential miskeys. That included building measures such as outlier ratios, severity scoring, and other analytical metrics designed to identify records that behave differently from the surrounding data.

The reporting has helped identify invoices requiring review and has contributed to the successful cancellation and correction of more than $1 million in invoices.

Controls & Data Analysis

PCard Duplicate Analysis

Creating visibility where we didn't have it before.

PCard activity creates a unique duplicate-payment challenge. The PCard system behaves much more like a bank statement, while AP contains the invoice and payment activity we would normally use for duplicate review.

During the latest audit, I identified that PCard transactions represented a significant source of duplicate-payment risk that previous review methods had not fully captured.

The problem was that the two sets of data weren't designed to match each other. Vendor names can be formatted differently between systems, so an exact match isn't enough.

I began developing a process to compare PCard and AP payment activity using fuzzy matching and approximate vendor-name matching, along with other transaction criteria.

The result has given us visibility into an area where we previously had very little ability to systematically compare the two payment streams.
