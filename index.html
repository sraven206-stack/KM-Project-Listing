Financial Analysis & Recovery

AP Open Credit Analysis

Because taking a credit shouldn't require the stars to align.

Our payment system is very protective of credits — sometimes a little too protective. A credit won't automatically apply against invoices unless several pieces of information line up exactly the way the system expects.

That creates an interesting problem: we can have a perfectly valid credit available to us, but one small difference somewhere in the process can keep it from actually being used.

I built an analysis process that helps me identify credits that are available to take but aren't moving through the normal payment process. From there, I can determine what doesn't align and where the process has broken down.

Instead of treating open credits like a mystery, the report gives me a place to start: here's the credit, here's why it isn't being used, and here's what needs attention so we can get our money.

Risk & Analytics

Miskey Invoice Risk Reporting

Data analytics at its finest.

Miskeys are one of the leading causes of the duplicate payments I see, but duplicates aren't the only risk. A small invoice-entry error can create payment issues, complicate vendor accounts, and cause problems much further down the road.

I wanted to find those errors without already knowing exactly what I was looking for.

I developed reporting that analyzes invoice activity for unusual patterns and potential miskeys. That included creating metrics such as outlier ratios, severity scoring, and other analytical measures to identify records that behave differently from the surrounding data.

The analysis has contributed to the successful cancellation and correction of more than $1 million worth of invoices.

Controls & Data Analysis

PCard Duplicate Analysis

Creating visibility where we didn't have it before.

PCard activity creates a unique duplicate-payment problem. The PCard system behaves much more like a bank statement, while AP contains the invoice and payment activity we would normally use for duplicate review.

During the latest audit, I identified that PCard transactions were a major source of duplication that previous review methods had not fully captured.

The problem was that the two sets of data weren't designed to match each other. Even something as basic as a vendor name can look completely different between the two systems.

I began developing a process to compare PCard and AP activity using fuzzy matching, approximate vendor-name matching, and other transaction criteria instead of relying on exact matches.

That has given us visibility into an area where we previously had essentially no systematic way to compare the two payment streams.
